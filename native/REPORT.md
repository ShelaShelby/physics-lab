**2. Saturn's rings: each part at its own slant**
- Geometry (ring_draw.h ringMu): the camera is at one height h over the plane, so mu = h / |P - C| = h / sqrt(h^2 + D^2); in 3D the face seen is the camera's side of the plane (ringCameraOnPoleSide), the 2D map keeps |pz|. Single scattering in a thin slab as before; the app says it (About, Chandrasekhar's entry).
- Design, said as settled: a table over mu was tried first (the 717 bands' means at 8, 12, 16 knots of ln mu, linear: errors 2.3 / 4.9 %, 0.95 / 2.0 %, 0.52 / 1.1 % of opacity / light; cubic worse; 16 knots would be 92 KB of uniforms, the card's range is 64 KB): not used. Instead exact: the card gets the profile's 7,884 optical depths (31.5 KB of uniforms) and each band's measurements (i0, n, cover); every pixel takes the mean of 1 - exp(-tau / mu) and of albedo / 0.5 x the slab formula over its band's 11 to 15 measurements at its own mu (kRingsFS; the dark face's difference of two exponentials written as tau / mu exp(-tau / mu) phi(tau (1/mu - 1/mu0)), no cancellation in single precision). Equal weights per 10 km measurement (the spacing varies 9.96-10.04 km): the area weights differ by at most 0.4 % of a term (measured: 5e-5). Bands no longer merge (orbit/ring_bands.cpp, now in orbit_core). The processor does the same at the corners of its triangles (blended across each); the reference picture (ringPictureCpu) per pixel.
- Tests (orbit_tests, test_rings075.inc, written first; 1943 checks, 0 failed): the app's mu within 1e-9 of |n . (P - C)| / |P - C| from the camera's vectors and of h / sqrt(h^2 + D^2), straight down and three tilted poles (near 0.116, far 0.083 where 0.75.0 gave 0.070 at both; 0.472 / 0.341 against 0.598; 0.709 / 0.504 against 0.830); a pixel's ring point on its ray; opacity and light of 5,454 pixels against the area means (limits 0.004 / 0.012 set before looking; got 5e-5); from 400 radii away 27,718 ring pixels, 0 over 8 levels from 0.75.0's one slant (largest 6). They fail on 0.75.0 (no ringMu; one slant).
- Card check: five views now (a fifth, 30 degrees below the plane): 0.00, 0.03, 0.02, 0.00, 0.03 % over 8 levels (limit 1 %). orbit.flyby, card against processor (multisampling off, validation on, 0 errors): 0.004 % of the pixels (0.75.0: 0.042 %).
- ui check ringslant (check_orbit_ui, ui_orbit): from the logged ring plane and the saved profile, by pinhole arithmetic written in the check: 9 x 9 blocks at the B ring's near and far lit points, within 8 levels (limit set before looking): near mu 0.732 expected (134.6, 122.3, 96.2) picture (135.1, 123.2, 96.6); far mu 0.365 expected (174.2, 158.6, 125.4) picture (170.6, 155.2, 123.4): the far, grazing part is brighter. **3. The benchmark's card times**
- Tagged: each timed frame carries its number (VkBackend::benchMark(tag), benchCollect's tags); a result is counted only if its frame was counted, dropped if it was left out (benchmark.cpp settle). That alone did not pass blackhole.moving_gas under LP_NUM_THREADS=1 (GPU 1.2 s a frame against counted frames of 0.18 s): measured, vkGetQueryPoolResults on lavapipe waits for every frame queued after it (0.9 s a read), all of it counted as the benchmark's own numbers, so nearly every frame was left out. Now each frame's command buffer copies its two timestamps into a host-visible buffer (vkCmdCopyQueryPoolResults) and they are read from memory once the fence has signalled: moving_gas under LP_NUM_THREADS=1: 39 frames, 312 ms a frame, GPU 307 ms, 1 frame left out (before: 53 left out of 108, GPU 696 ms "not reliable"). The ROADMAP lines for this and for the 260 ms reads are deleted.
