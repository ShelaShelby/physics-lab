# Native Physics Lab 0.10.2 (alpha) — final report (stages 1 to 3)

Atom Lab only: phase colours, quantum beats, smooth photon jumps. Older reports are in git history. `index.html` was not touched. Version, 🕰 history entry, README, ROADMAP (0.10.2 done), RESUME, OWNER-TODO and About are updated.

## What changed
- **Stage 1: phase colours.** Complex orbitals m = ±1 … ±l of p, d and f (Condon-Shortley; own ids ≥ 1000, the 30 real orbitals untouched), "Phase colours: Sign / Wheel" (default Sign = today's look; Wheel = arg ψ on a constant-lightness CIELAB hue circle, colour-ring legend top right), m choices in Orientation (complex states always use the wheel). Stage 1 tested: winding 2πm to 2e-14, L_z to 6e-10, orthonormal to 4e-14, Y_lm against SciPy 1.17.1 to 9e-16, wheel against scikit-image 0.26.0 to 1.4e-4, real dots bit for bit as in 0.10.1.
- **Stage 2: quantum beats.** "Superposition Off / On" (second orbital + orientation, Mix 0 to 100 %): ψ = a ψA e^(−iEA t/ħ) + b ψB e^(−iEB t/ħ), energies from `rydM` (reduced mass); same n: no beats, said in the text (no fine structure, no Lamb shift). Screen beat 4 s at Playback speed 5; the readout gives "real beat period 406 as, slowed by …". Ring memory is 3.12 s at every density (16 dots/step, 3,840 dots/s at Normal), so instead of a shorter memory the dots stay fixed samples of q = (|A|²+|B|²)/2 and each is shown with probability |ψ|²/(2q) (exact thinning). Tests: period vs CODATA h/(E2−E1) 2.7e-10; ⟨1s|z|2p⟩ = 128√2/243 a0 by my own integral; norm; dots within 4 standard errors, chi-square 20 to 23.
- **Stage 3: smooth photon jumps** (`atom_sim.cpp`, `atom_core.*`, `atom_view.cpp`, `atom_panel.cpp`).
  - **Cloud views.** ψ = e^(iφu)(cosθ A + sinθ B e^(−iφ)), θ = π/2·smoothstep(t/1.2 s), φ = the slowed beat phase (4 s per beat at speed 5), so the cloud slides and sloshes. The stage-2 recipe alone (one ring sampled from q) would reshuffle every dot in the first frame, because the old ring holds dots of the old orbital only. So I use **two fixed populations**: the old cloud, and 24,000 (any density) new dots of the target orbital made at the start, hidden at first. Each dot is shown with fractions fA, fB with |A|²fA + |B|²fB = |ψ|² exactly and 0 ≤ f ≤ 1 (Cauchy-Schwarz), blending "old dots fill first" (weight cos²θ) and "new dots fill first" (sin²θ): at the start exactly the old dots, at the end exactly the new ones, which then become the ring. No resampling after the start, no swap. The colour gauge multiplies ψ by e^(iφu) so the end colours are the new orbital's own (no hue jump). Cost here (software, one core): set-up once 4.5 / 7.2 / 15.7 ms (Normal / High / Ultra), then 2.2 / 4.7 / 9.1 ms per frame.
  - **Orbit view (Modern and Bohr 1913).** The radius glides from n1²a0/Z to n2²a0/Z over 0.8 s (smoothstep), turning at the blended circular rates; the landing flash is kept. Text: Bohr's model has no path between circles; the glide only helps the eye.
  - **Photon.** Emit: the jump first, the photon appears in the very step it ends; absorb: the jump starts in the very step the photon arrives. Energies, wavelengths, selection rules, `manualJump` messages unchanged. Jumps stay off while Superposition is on and for complex states; the jump uses its own state and never touches the user's Superposition setting.
  - **90 % outline:** fades out over the first 0.25 s of a jump, hidden, fades back in over 0.4 s after landing. **Reduced motion** (as the camera does elsewhere: jumps to the end): the jump is over at once, an emitted photon appears at once, an absorbed one still flies in.
  - **Text** under Photons: a textbook picture of a transition (mixing of two states), not a calculation of how a photon is absorbed; the screen is slowed; no real jump time is given (no named source). About: Griffiths (time-dependent perturbation theory, two-level system; **book not opened here**) and the CODATA Bohr radius (test only, from knowledge).
  - Hook `PL_ATOM_DO_FRAME=n` presses `PL_ATOM_DO` at frame n.
- **The web-comparison runs** (3 runs with jumps) still compare word for word: they set `AtomLab::webJumps = true`, which keeps the web's instant leap and dot-by-dot morph. The app never sets it; the runs and their saved references are unchanged. That keeps a code path used only by tests.

## Tests (full suite once: `orbit_tests` 1,377, `atom_tests` 17,936, `app_tests` 1,383, `nbody_tests` 36; all pass)
- **Fractions:** 20,000 random cases: identity to 2.5e-16, within [0, 1], endpoints (limit 1e-9: cos(π/2) is 6e-17 in floating point).
- **Dots (a, c):** 120 full rings, every 400th dot with its own random number (z autocorrelation falls like e^(−lag/60)): at the start all 1.44 million old dots shown and identical to those on screen before the jump, none of the new; at the end none of the old. Mean r within 4 standard errors at u = 0, ¼, ½, ¾, 1 (largest 1.6 σ); radial chi-square 11.6 to 24.7 (19 dof, limit 48).
- **Norm (b):** along the jump |∫|ψ|² − 1| ≤ 6.3e-6 (1e-3). **Continuity (c):** exact ⟨r⟩ (1.5 and 5 a0 by my own integral) changes ≤ 0.033 of the gap per 1/60 s (limit 0.1); the full integral matches cos²θ rA + sin²θ rB to 3e-5.
- **Orbit (d):** 192 steps = 0.8 s, monotone, no overshoot, Z = 1 and 2, up and down. Landing radius within 5e-4 of n²a0/Z (one leapfrog step of wobble, measured 1.3e-4; exact to 1e-9 in the reduced-motion case with no leapfrog after).
- **(e) Hand-over and robustness:** emit/absorb timing exact in both views; Auto photons 60 s (15 and 18 jumps): never a photon and a jump together, every hand-over exact, Orbit radius change ≤ 0.117 a0 per 1/240 s (limit 0.15 = 1.5 × biggest gap/192); paused frames (`tick` only) do not move a jump (the app does not call `step` while paused); changing orbital, element or view mid-jump resets cleanly; Superposition setting untouched. **(f)** Jumps work after Superposition on and off. Outline fade checked.
- **Limits that were my own choices:** 5e-4 landing (reason above), 0.15 a0 per step, the outline fade times. Two test errors of mine were fixed, not loosened: the endpoint tolerance, and measuring the landing radius after the next photon flight.
- **Existing doubtful value:** the lab's Bohr radius is the web's rounded 0.529 Å, 0.034 % below CODATA's 0.529177 Å (5.29177210903e-11 m, written from knowledge); every Orbit radius inherits it. Left as is, as the web has it.
