# Native Physics Lab 0.10.4 (alpha) — stage 2 report (finishes 0.10.4)

The real Moon in the Orbit Lab, **Planet physics Real only**. Simple, the other moons and the Atom Lab are unchanged. Older reports are in git history. Version stays 0.10.4; history, README, ROADMAP, RESUME and OWNER-TODO are updated. No new owner decision, so DECISIONS is as it was (my own choices are listed here).

## What changed
- **Earth and Moon** (`solar_system.*`): the N-body "Earth" is the Earth-Moon barycentre (EMB). In Real, with g = the ELP2000-82B vector Earth to Moon and mu = GM_Moon / GM_Earth: **Moon = EMB + g/(1+mu), Earth = EMB - g mu/(1+mu)**. mu = 4902.800118 / 398600.435507 = 0.0123000, GMs read from JPL's Astrodynamic Parameters page itself on 2026-10-03 (DE440; `kRealGMEarthAlone`, `kRealGMMoon` in `nbody.*`; `kRealGM[3]` untouched). Ignored (in the code comment): ELP's own mass ratio, not looked up; the effect is its relative difference from the page's times Earth's 4,300-5,000 km offset (5 m if they differ by 1e-6). Earth's velocity, and so its orbit numbers on the card, stay the barycentre's (the Earth card says so in Real); Earth's trail follows the barycentre. The Moon is worked out once per frame, not per sub-step (3 ELP calls: position and the orbit plane from a half-hour difference).
- **Only Earth's Moon** is real, and only 1800-2100 (`realMoon()`); other moons, Simple, and Real outside those years use the old Kepler Moon, and the card says so.
- **Drawing** (`orbit_view.cpp`): the Moon is at its real direction at the app's usual exaggerated distance (label unchanged), scaled by real distance / mean distance, so perigee and apogee show and at true scale it is the real distance. The ring is the Moon's instantaneous orbit plane through the Moon (not the old tilted circle). No phase drawing (the disc is lit towards the Sun as before).
- **Card** (`card_text.cpp`): "Phase now: waning gibbous, 90% lit", the angle from the Sun, the real distance now, the source and 5.2 km. `moonPhase()` in `moon_elp.*`: lit fraction = (1 - cos of the geocentric elongation) / 2, first order (the exact phase angle differs by up to 0.15 degrees, up to 0.0013 in the fraction); waxing/waning from the longitude difference; names by lit fraction (new under 2 %, crescent to 40, quarter to 60, gibbous to 98, full above): my convention.
- **Real note, About, history, jump message**: note now says what the Moon is, the measured error and limits; About entries updated (ELP now used, Horizons Earth table added); the "moons start at made-up places" jump message says Earth's Moon is real when it is. `main.cpp`: `PL_SELECT=moon:Earth` for automatic checks. New: `tools/make_earth_ref.py`, `tests/reference/earth_table.inc` (Horizons Earth 399 from the Sun 10, 301 rows, DE441, retrieved 2026-10-03, query in the header), `tests/test_moon_real.cpp`.

## Measured (moon_real_tests)
- **Earth against Horizons' Earth (399)**, 301 dates 1800-2100, heliocentric: worst **3,959.9 km** (relativity off, mean 1,654 km) and **22,080.1 km** (on, mean 9,227 km). Same as the barycentre's 0.10.3 numbers (3,960 and 22,081, still quoted in the note; a test checks they are Earth's maxima rounded up). Guards 4,000 and 22,301 km (about 1 % above, regression guards).
- Earth is 4,335 to 4,940 km from the barycentre (test: 4,300-5,000, from the known distance range 356,000-407,000 km). Moon seen from Earth against the ELP vector: 2.9e-5 m; Earth and Moon balance on the N-body EMB: 3.1e-5 m (limit 1e-3 m: double rounding of 1.5e11 m numbers); stored barycentre equals the clock's exactly.
- **The seven other planets** (both relativity settings): none worse than its 0.10.3 number (for example Mercury 29,230.1 against 29,231 km, 70,044.3 against 70,045).
- Phases at Horizons' full moon (JD 2461043.91990): "full moon", 99.86 % lit, 175.7 degrees; new moon (JD 2461029.57308) 0.18 % (the Moon is 4.8 degrees off the ecliptic); quarters 50.00 %; Horizons' own Moon and Sun vectors give the same name and 99.86 %; one cycle sampled every 0.25 day passes all 8 names in order (8 changes), brightest 99.8 %.
- Earth/Moon mass ratio from the page 81.3005682 against Horizons' header 81.3005691: 1.04e-8. **My first limit (1e-8) was a guess and too tight**; the header's data are dated 2018 and the page is DE440, so I set 1e-7 (it only catches a wrong value; 1e-7 moves the offset by 0.5 mm).

## Time speed (item 5), what I chose
The Moon goes round 8 times a real second at Time speed 10^0.575 (3.76 x the base, 218 days a second). Above that the drawing (rule unchanged, the same for every moon) already hides the Moon and lights the whole orbit. I added: Earth is then held at the barycentre (its 4,300-5,000 km wobble would only shake) and its trail never had the wobble. At or below 8 laps a second the Moon is drawn as before. Tests check the switch at 8 laps, Earth at the barycentre when fast and 4,300-5,000 km off when slow, and that the Moon vector follows the date. Flicker itself I could not judge from stills: it is on the owner's list.

## Doubtful points (not changed)
- The jump message "After 2050 the planets' places are extrapolated beyond NASA's table" is not true in Real (JPL start, integrated); it is old text.
- The phase is geometric (no light time, TDB used as the date); the names' thresholds are mine; "lit %" ignores libration and the Moon's real brightness.
- Earth is drawn at barycentre minus the Moon's share but moves with the barycentre's velocity: its osculating numbers on the card mix the two (position offset 3e-5 of the Sun distance); not checked at the digits shown.
- The Moon's 5.2 km (2.9") is the stage-1 error at 170 dates, not measured between them; Earth's error against Horizons is dominated by the barycentre's 3,960 km.
