# Native Physics Lab 0.10.5 (alpha) — stage 3 report: Orbit Lab data fixes

Rows 3, 4, 9 and 12 of the stage 1 audit (`git show 5239fcd:native/REPORT.md`). **The AU (kAU) was not touched.** Sources read on 2026-10-03: JPL Planetary Physical Parameters page, Horizons API (Sun header, vectors, 67P elements), SBDB API. All reachable. Version stays 0.10.5; history, ROADMAP, RESUME, OWNER-TODO and About updated.

## What changed
- **Row 3, gravity.** `PlanetData` now holds JPL's "Equatorial Gravity" in m/s²: Mercury 3.70, Venus 8.87, Earth 9.80, Mars 3.71, Jupiter 24.79, Saturn 10.44, Uranus 8.87, Neptune 11.15. The card's "g" is the planet's value over Earth's 9.80 on the same page (no outside constant). The page defines the value as "the gravitational acceleration on the planet's surface at the equator" and says nothing about a 1-bar level, so the card says only **"gravity 2.5 g at the equator"** (not for dwarf planets: theirs is still mass/radius², unchanged). Jupiter 2.36 → 2.53 g, Saturn 0.92 → 1.07, Uranus 0.89 → 0.90, Neptune 1.12 → 1.14; the four inner planets move under 0.4 %.
- **Row 4, Sun radius.** `kSunRadius` = 695,700 km, with Horizons' header lines quoted in the comment ("Vol. mean radius, km = 695700", "Solar radius (IAU2015)= 695700 km"). The Sun card computes its radius from the constant; two more typed 696000 (the Sun tile's zoom, the Learn button's zoom) now use it too.
- **Row 12, comets.** Each comet takes q, e, i, Ω, ω, tp and period from one record, named in a comment with its id, epoch and solution date: Halley SBDB 75 (epoch 1968-01-20), Hale-Bopp 226 (2022-09-15), NEOWISE 31 (2020-07-06), Encke K273/20 (2023-07-22), Swift-Tuttle 32 (1995-10-10). **The old values were mixed**: for 67P i, q, e were from 2021 but Ω and ω from 2015 (Ω 50.1° vs 36.3° at 2021); Hale-Bopp's q (0.8907 vs 0.8905) and NEOWISE's period (2,443,000 vs 2,478,985 d) matched no record.
- **67P, my choice.** SBDB's current record (K284/1) has its epoch in 2015; as a fixed orbit it is 1.37 AU off Horizons in Nov 2021 and 0.15 AU in Jan 2026. I used the same solution K284/1 osculating at 2021-11-02 (the 2021 perihelion), asked from Horizons (ELEMENTS): 0.011 AU off in Jan 2026, and still one solution at one epoch. The old mixed values: 0.41 AU.
- **Halley's note and tile.** With SBDB's period (27,728 d) the next closest approach on the card is 8 Jan 2062, JPL's prediction is 28 Jul 2061. The note says both; the "Halley returns, 2061" tile says the lab's orbit has it about 5 months later. (The old period had been tuned to hit 2061.)
- **Row 9, Simple note.** One added sentence: fixed orbit, and the worst error against Horizons 1800-2100 in million km: Saturn 8.0, Uranus 2.9, Neptune 2.3, Jupiter 1.8, Mars 0.09, Earth 0.016, Venus 0.014, Mercury 0.0076, with "JPL's table is made for 1800-2050". The numbers are `kSimpleErrKm` in `real_errors.h`.
- **About**: the fact-sheet line no longer says gravity; new lines for the JPL parameters page, the Horizons Sun header, and the comet records and vectors.
