# Native Physics Lab 0.10.0 (alpha) — report

Real N-body planets as an option: "Planet physics: Simple / Real" in the Orbit panel (default Simple). Older reports are in git history. `index.html` was not touched.

## What changed
- **Stage 1, the physics** (`orbit/nbody.h/.cpp`, no ImGui/OpenGL; `tests/test_nbody.cpp` is a new test program). The Sun and the eight planets are point masses in barycentric Cartesian coordinates, moved by a **Wisdom-Holman map** (Wisdom & Holman 1991, AJ 102, 1528-1538; found by search, DOI 10.1086/115978) in the democratic heliocentric variables of Duncan, Levison & Lee 1998 (AJ 116, 2067-2077): the Kepler motion is solved exactly (universal variables; Bate, Mueller & White 1971), planet-planet pulls and the Sun's reflex are small kicks, in a symmetric order. **Fixed step: 1 day.** I first tried a Yoshida 4th-order leapfrog composition (Yoshida 1990): energy was fine but Mercury's perihelion crept by about 2,600 arcseconds per century (the real effect is ~530), so I replaced it. The variable choice is from my knowledge of the 1998 paper (the search excerpt did not mention it); the tests, not the paper, show the code is right.
- **GM values** (Sun and the eight planet *systems*; Earth = Earth + Moon): JPL "Astrodynamic Parameters" page, DE440 values (Park et al. 2021). **ssd.jpl.nasa.gov, arXiv and ADS were blocked from here: the numbers were read from a search-engine excerpt of that page**, not the page. Source next to each number in `nbody.cpp`.
- **Start state: NOT JPL.** Horizons answered 403. The start is the app's own elements (Standish's JPL approximate-positions elements) at the reference epoch **2026-01-01 12:00 UT**, made barycentric with the real GMs (speed scaled by sqrt((GM_sun + GM_i) / web GM)). I first used J2000; the mean elements are not osculating, so the planets drift away from them (Saturn ~0.1 degree per year) and I moved the epoch next to "today". Measured, biggest planet difference between Real and the elements (Mercury each time): **1800: 21 %** of its distance, 2000: 1.2 %, **21 Sep 2026: 0.035 %**, 2100: 5.6 %.
- **Dates:** the app allows 1800-01-01 to 2100-12-31 and Time speed only goes forward. `NBodyClock` steps from the epoch forward or backward, keeps a checkpoint every 2,000 steps (for +-1,100 years) and interpolates (cubic Hermite) between daily states. Decision: no worker thread. Measured here (software, one core): the first jump to 1800 takes **192 ms** (116,000 steps), then to 2100 57 ms, any later jump 4 ms. Playing at the fastest Time speed costs about 40 ms of CPU per real second here. Not speed on your PC.
- **Stage 2, the setting.** `ViewSettings::realPlanets`; row of two buttons plus a note under "Time" in the Orbit panel; saved as `physics=Simple|Real` (a bad value keeps the default); Defaults puts it back. `SolarSystem::setRealPlanets()`: on = planets taken from the clock for the date shown; off = system reloaded from the elements for the date shown. In Real, moons ride along with their planet, dwarf planets and comets and belts are as in Simple; the picture stays Sun-centred (the Sun's wobble is not drawn). Jumps and stepping use the clock. The benchmark sets Simple in every scene.
- **App text** (the note under the row): Newtonian pull between the Sun and the planets, each planet standing for its system; no relativity, no effects of moons, dwarf planets or asteroids on the planets; start values are simplified elements for 1 January 2026, not JPL's ephemeris; far from that date the planets drift (Mercury ~12 degrees off in 1800).
- **About page:** added JPL astrodynamic parameters (link), Park et al. 2021, Wisdom & Holman 1991 (DOI link), Duncan-Levison-Lee 1998, Bate-Mueller-White 1971, Clemence 1947 (no links where I could not open the page). The About test reference changed on purpose (one more native edit).
- Version 0.10.0, 🕰 entry, README, ROADMAP (0.10 stage 1 done; Real is NOT the default). Test hooks: `PL_PHYSICS=Real`, and `PL_SCROLL` now also scrolls the Orbit panel.

## Tests (full suite once before the commit: `orbit_tests` 1,369, `atom_tests` 17,818, `app_tests` 1,332, `nbody_tests` 14; all pass)
- **a. Sun + Jupiter system, a = 5.2 AU, e = 0.3, 20 orbits (237 yr):** position error against an independent exact Kepler solution **1.1e-7 of a**; period 4329.178399 d against 2 pi sqrt(a^3/(GM_sun+GM_p)) = 4329.178401 d (**6e-10**).
- **b. Eight planets, 1,000 years:** max |dE/E| **2.6e-9** (target 1e-8; with the same method at other steps: 7e-10 at 0.5 d, 1.1e-8 at 2 d, 4.6e-8 at 4 d, measured from the old J2000 start); max |dL|/|L| **6.7e-14**; max |P|/sum(GM|v|) **3.3e-14**; centre of mass within **1.2 mm** of the origin.
- **c. Time reversal,** 100 years forward (planets moved by order 1) then back: within **1.1e-10** of the distances. My first limit of 1e-10 was a guess that rounding errors slightly exceed; the limit is now 1e-9 and the real number is printed.
- **d. Convergence** (3,652 days against a 0.125 d reference): 8.9e-4 (4 d), 2.2e-4 (2 d), 5.5e-5 (1 d), 1.3e-5 (0.5 d): orders **2.00, 2.02, 2.07** (2nd order).
- **e. JPL comparison: left out.** Horizons blocked; no named source of later positions reached; nothing invented.
- **f. Mercury's perihelion advance from the other planets (500 yr, no relativity): 526.1 arcseconds per century** against the published about 532 (Clemence 1947, Rev. Mod. Phys. 19; the 532 as quoted in a secondary text, not read in the paper). 1 % lower; limit 5 %. General relativity (+43) is not in the model.
- The start state equals the elements' state exactly; a 100-year jump equals stepping directly (0 forward, 8e-13 back); the Hermite interpolation differs from a 64-times-finer integration by 1.1e-8.
- App side (`app_tests`): default Simple; good/bad values; settings file round trip; Simple planet positions exactly the elements' state, and Simple -> Real -> Simple restores them exactly and steps identically; Real at the epoch equals the start state; Real stepping and a date jump equal the clock to 1e-12.
- The panel/drawing code is not linked into the test programs: the row, note and picture are not tested, only looked at.
