## The binding height (a chosen scale; stated in `chart_view.h`, the legend, About)
**Height = (binding energy per nucleon in keV) / 9,000, cut to 0..1: linear, bottom 0, top 9,000 keV.** Per nucleon, not total (the total grows with A and hides the peak). Bottom 0 because zero binding is the honest baseline (another would exaggerate the fall); top a round number above the data's highest (8,794.56 keV), so the peak stands at 0.977. 28 tiles per scale height, chosen by looking (16 left the peak and the fall unreadable).
**Data.** The lab's binding table (`kBeaLines`, whole keV) was used as the check: it agrees with AME2020 for all 2,549 measured values to 0.4998 keV (rounding). What it lacked is **which values are estimates**, so I fetched AME2020: `https://www-nds.iaea.org/amdc/ame2020/mass.mas20.txt`, 2026-10-04, HTTP 200, **476,239 bytes**, Last-Modified 7 Mar 2021, **sha256 05b054a0…c674**, `data/ame/` + README (tool `make_ame_data.py`; citation as the file gives it: Huang et al., Chin. Phys. C45, 030002 and Wang et al., 030003, 2021). The file defines "# in place of the decimal point: estimated (non-experimental)". Its 3,557 nuclides are exactly the lab's; **2,549 measured, 1,008 estimated**.
**The peak, READ from the table by the program (`ameHighest`), not typed:** **Z 28, N 34 = nickel-62, 8,794.5555 keV**, then Z 26 N 32 (iron-58) 8,792.2534 and Z 26 N 30 (iron-56) 8,790.3563. The app's legend and the peak note take their words and numbers from it. **Finding:** AME2020's **measured masses end at Z = 110**; Z 111-118 (nuclides NUBASE2020 lists, with half-lives) have only estimated masses.
**Nuclides without a value:** none lack a row (0 "unknown"). The **1,008 estimates get no height**: flat, pale (stage 3's language for extrapolated half-lives), never a number. H-1 is measured at exactly 0 (a flat solid block); the 2 negative values (Li-3, B-6) are estimates.

## Switching the height changes nothing else
Tested directly (both modes drawn and compared): the ground plate, the 13 magic bands' 28 triangles and 56 edge lines are at **exactly the same places**; every block is the same nuclide, class, order and x; the top colour of the more than 2,000 blocks that are solid in both is the same; the status line, the hover words of all 3,557 nuclides, the selection and tilt/zoom/pan are identical; the chosen nuclide's pin is at the same x. **To make that true I changed the fit:** it now uses the grid up to the taller of the two scales, not the blocks' own heights, so switching does not rescale. **Moved on purpose:** in height-limited windows the half-life landscape is **about 8 % smaller than in stage 3** (K 0.821 vs 0.897 at 1280×720; unchanged at 900×700).

## The valley, and the island that is a prediction
**Valley:** the band of stable and very long-lived nuclides, measured: named on the chart ("valley of stability (measured)", a dot and leader at the median stable tile, found in the data: Z 47 N 62) and in the notes box ("measured, not predicted: nuclides on either side decay back towards it").
**Where the measured chart ends:** the heaviest tiles, Z 118 (N 175-177), ringed and labelled "heaviest known: Z = 118 (oganesson); nothing observed beyond"; both read from the data.
**Island: NO source reached, so nothing specific is drawn or quoted.** Tried (plain GET): Wikipedia (page and API), arXiv (abstract and API), INSPIRE, Crossref, OpenAlex, Semantic Scholar, Europe PMC, HAL, CDS, Zenodo, ResearchGate, Springer, APS, ScienceDirect, AIP, Annual Reviews, IOP, Nature, Britannica, scholar.archive.org, web.archive.org, NNDC, OSTI, JINR, the Flerov lab, GSI, LBL, Google, DuckDuckGo, Bing: about 30 hosts, **every one "CONNECT tunnel failed, response 403"** (the proxy's policy denial; its README says not to retry). Only the IAEA AME file answered. **The app says (notes box, always):** "Island of stability: a PREDICTION, not something seen. Theory expects a region of superheavy nuclei beyond the heaviest known (Z = 118) that might live far longer than their neighbours. None of them has been observed, and where it lies is disputed.
