# Native Physics Lab 0.10 (alpha) — report

Real N-body planets as an option ("Planet physics: Simple / Real"). Older reports are in git history. `index.html` was not touched.

## Stage 1: the physics and its tests (no screen change yet)
- **New files** `orbit/nbody.h/.cpp` (no ImGui, no OpenGL) and `tests/test_nbody.cpp` (new test program `nbody_tests`). `orbit_tests` (1,369), `atom_tests` (17,818) and `app_tests` (1,321) are unchanged and pass. The planets' elements-to-state code was moved into `planetStateAt()` in `solar_system.cpp` (same maths; `loadAt` calls it).
- **Integrator: a Wisdom-Holman map** (Wisdom & Holman 1991, The Astronomical Journal 102, 1528-1538; checked by a search, DOI 10.1086/115978) in the democratic heliocentric variables of Duncan, Levison & Lee 1998 (AJ 116, 2067-2077). The Kepler motion of each planet is solved exactly (universal variables, Stumpff functions: Bate, Mueller & White, *Fundamentals of Astrodynamics*, 1971), the Sun's own motion and the planet-planet pulls are small kicks, in a symmetric order: symplectic, time-reversible, 2nd order. **First I tried a Yoshida 4th-order composition of leapfrog steps** (Yoshida 1990, Physics Letters A 150, 262): its energy was fine, but Mercury's perihelion crept by about 2,600 arcseconds per century at a half-day step (the real planetary effect is about 530), so I replaced it. The Duncan-Levison-Lee splitting is from my knowledge of that paper; the search excerpt did not mention the variables, and the tests, not the paper, are what show the code is right.
- **Step: 1 day, fixed.** Largest relative energy error over 1,000 years: 7.0e-10 (0.5 d), **2.8e-9 (1 d)**, 1.1e-8 (2 d), 4.6e-8 (4 d), 1.9e-7 (8 d). One day meets the 1e-8 target.
- **GM values** (m^3/s^2): the Sun and the eight planet *systems* (Earth = Earth + Moon, Mars ... Neptune: system values) from the JPL "Astrodynamic Parameters" page, the DE440 values (Park et al. 2021, AJ). **ssd.jpl.nasa.gov, arXiv and ADS were blocked from this session; the numbers were read from a search-engine excerpt of that page** (a search without numbers in it), not from the page itself. The source is next to each number in `nbody.cpp`. If you want them checked against the page, that is a ten-minute job on a normal PC.
- **Starting state: NOT from JPL.** JPL Horizons (ssd-api.jpl.nasa.gov) answered 403 from here. The start is the app's own orbital elements (Standish's JPL approximate-positions elements, 1800-2050) at J2000 turned into barycentric positions and velocities with the real GMs (each planet's speed scaled by sqrt((GM_sun + GM_i) / the web's GM) so its orbit keeps the elements' shape). So **in Real mode the planets drift from their true positions over decades and more; no JPL accuracy is claimed.**
- **Dates and jumps.** The app allows 1800-01-01 to 2100-12-31 (J2000 is inside), and Time speed only goes forward. `NBodyClock` steps from J2000 forward or backward with a checkpoint every 2,000 steps (kept for +-1,100 years) and cubic-Hermite interpolation between daily states. Measured here (software, one core): the very first jump to 1800 takes **155 ms** (115,000 steps), then to 2100 72 ms, and a second jump anywhere near either 2.5-5 ms. I decided to do it in the calling thread (no worker): a single 0.15 s pause on the first far jump is acceptable; this is not speed on your PC.

## Tests of stage 1 (`nbody_tests`, 14 checks, all pass; the real numbers)
- **Sun + one planet** (Jupiter's GM, a = 5.2 AU, e = 0.3, 20 orbits = 237 years): worst position error against an independent exact Kepler solution **1.1e-7 of a**; period 4329.178399 d against 2 pi sqrt(a^3 / (GM_sun + GM_planet)) = 4329.178401 d (**6e-10**).
- **Eight planets, 1,000 years:** max |dE/E| = **2.8e-9**; max |dL|/|L| = **5.8e-14**; max |P| / sum(GM |v|) = **2.9e-14** (start 1e-16); the centre of mass stays within **1.6 mm** of the origin.
- **Time reversal:** 100 years forward (the planets moved by order 1 of their distances) and 100 back: within **4.6e-11**.
- **Step convergence** (3,652 days against a 0.125 d reference; Mercury's position as fraction of its distance): 9.0e-4 (4 d), 2.2e-4 (2 d), 5.5e-5 (1 d), 1.3e-5 (0.5 d): orders **2.00, 2.02, 2.07**, as for a 2nd-order scheme.
- **Mercury's perihelion advance from the other planets, 500 years, no relativity:** **526.3 arcseconds per century** against the published "about 532" (Clemence 1947, Reviews of Modern Physics 19; the 532 as quoted in a secondary text, I did not read Clemence's own number): 1% lower, test limit 5%. General relativity (about 43) is not in the model.
- The start state equals the app's elements (positions 1e-16, scaled velocities); the clock returns it exactly at J2000; a 100-year jump equals stepping directly (0 forward, 6e-13 back); the Hermite interpolation differs from a 64-times-finer integration by 7e-9.
- **Left out:** the comparison with JPL positions after 1 and 10 years (no reachable named source of positions; Horizons blocked). Nothing was invented in its place.
- **Sources in the About page** (all added in this stage): JPL astrodynamic parameters (link), Park et al. 2021 (no link), Wisdom & Holman 1991 (DOI link), Duncan, Levison & Lee 1998, Bate-Mueller-White, Clemence 1947 (no links: not reachable). The "About" test reference was updated on purpose (one more edit, 6 About edits).
