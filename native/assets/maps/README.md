The map scripts are in `native/tools` (`map_shrink.py` reads only the rows it needs from the big USGS
TIFFs over HTTP with rasterio/GDAL, and averages them into the smaller size). Venus has no map: no true-colour map of its clouds exists (NASA's pictures are UV, violet or radar); it is drawn in its
measured colour (tools/map_albedo_colour.py). The OPAL files were downloaded by the owner (archive.stsci.edu cannot be verified
from the cloud) and sent in the chat on 5 Nov 2026.

0.50 (11 Nov 2026): the eight moon maps above, made by `map_moon.py` (reads only the rows it needs over HTTP, averages only valid pixels, fills never-imaged areas with the area-mean grey, as `map_pluto_fill.py` did; 1024×512 to keep the program small: 8 maps, 0.97 MB). The product pages were saved and read; every map is a simple-cylindrical GeoTIFF, east to the right, whose bounds give the middle longitude (0°, Rhea 180°). The moons are turned by their IAU models (src/orbit/iau_rotation.h). No measured colour of these moons was found, so all eight are black and white; Titan (haze), Enceladus (only an airbrushed map), Titania, Oberon and Deimos (no global mosaic in the USGS catalogue) stay grey balls, and their cards say "colour not measured here". No colour mosaic of all of Pluto was found (USGS has the black-and-white one used above).

0.77.0 (23 Nov 2026, outside review): **brightness.** Each map is multiplied by one factor so that the app's ball, fully lit, has the body's measured geometric albedo (V band; `tools/map_albedo_scale.py`, its output `tools/map_albedo_scale.txt`; `src/render/planet_maps.cpp mapAlbedo`). The USGS moon mosaics and the Moon Kit are display pictures, not reflectance (the USGS pages, saved: Io "empirically adjusted in brightness and contrast", Pluto "linearly stretched to 8bit"), so their factors are large (Ganymede 10.4, Pluto 7.7, Io 7.6) and only their mean brightness is measured; their contrast is the mosaic's. The OPAL maps (calibrated I/F, limb darkening taken out) did not match under the app's Lambert law: Saturn, Uranus and Neptune drew at 2/3 of their measured albedo; their factors are 1.36, 1.28, 1.29.

**Latitudes** (the shader takes every map as planetocentric): Jupiter (PIA07782): the page says "equal increments of planetocentric latitude", unchanged. Mars (USGS): planetocentric, stated on its page. Saturn, Uranus, Neptune: converted from planetographic by `map_opal.py`. Earth (Blue Marble): the saved page states no latitude type (geodetic and planetocentric latitude differ by at most 0.19°, worked out from WGS84's flattening 1/298.257: about one map pixel), unchanged. Mercury, the Moon, Pluto and the moons are drawn as balls, where the two latitudes are the same.
