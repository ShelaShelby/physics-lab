Each is under 190 KB (all 24: 2.9 MB). They are drawn with HTML and CSS in Chromium (`src/`, throwaway code); **the lab pictures inside them are real pictures from the real app**, drawn at the exact size each layout gives the picture (Save image, headless). The words and numbers are the app's own (Earth's card, the chart's legend counts, the buttons, "Half-life 4.47 billion years …"); nothing new is claimed. Colours are the app's own flat colours (Aero's lime and aqua as in 0.14). Free font **Inter** (OFL) and **Lucide** icons (ISC); licences in `assets/`. Rules: `../DESIGN.md`.

**About the two window sizes (the owner's small-text situation).** Both are drawn at Windows scale 100 % with the real text sizes of DESIGN.md (body 16 px, caption 13, title 20, display 28): text is NOT shrunk in the small window, the layout gives way instead. 1000x640 is a small, not maximised window. It is also what a 1500x960 window looks like at 150 % scale (the layout counts in units, not pixels), so a high-resolution screen at 125–150 % behaves like a small window. The real owner's setting is not known (OWNER-TODO asks). Labels drawn ON the lab pictures are the real app's 12 px; the rule would make them 13.

**What the mockups do NOT show:** only one tab of the panel at a time and only a sample of the controls (14–26 are shown; the Atom Lab has about 200 today), so they do not prove that everything fits; Learn, Achievements, Settings, About and the changelog are not drawn; the Atom Lab's element picker is not drawn; no mouse-over states; Aero's glass is approximated in CSS, not the app's renderer.

## A — left rail, big picture, one right panel with tabs (Choose · View · Info)
**Fixes:** it keeps today's idea (a picture and a side column) but ends the 4,857–5,936 px scroll: the column is cut into three tabs of one screen each, grouped Choose / View / Info; the header, the "Did you know?" bar and the tab bar are gone (the 163 px above the picture are now a 64 px rail on the left), so the picture is 60 % of the window (Atom chart today: 48 %); text is 16 px; the legend and the Earth card move out of the picture into the panel; the periodic table leaves the panel (a picker, not drawn). **Costs:** the panel takes 30 % of the width for ever, so the picture is never full-bleed and in a small window it is only 616 px wide with stat lines wrapping (see `A_*_1000x640`); the rail is icons only (names on hover), a small discoverability loss; tabs hide controls (one more click to reach View or Info). Cheapest to build: the panels stay, they are re-cut and restyled (about the same work as the style system itself). AniList-likeness: medium — space, few sizes, but details are not "on click", they are a tab.

## B — slim top bar, big picture, bottom drawer
**Fixes:** a 56 px top bar holds all navigation; the controls sit in a wide bottom drawer where Choose, View and Info are side by side, so nothing has to be scrolled to compare them, and the picture is as wide as the window (1280 px). **Costs:** the drawer takes 31 % of the height, so the picture is only 62 % of the window (56 % in the small one, 1000x360), and a tall picture such as the chart shrinks and leaves empty bands left and right (see `B_chart_*`); each column fits four or five rows, so the long Atom Lab lists need a scrolling column or more tabs inside the drawer (the mockup shows only a sample); in a small window the drawer drops to two columns. Good for wide pictures (Orbit Lab), worst for the Atom Lab's chart and clouds. A new layout system is needed (a docking bar), a medium cost. AniList-likeness: low — it is a dashboard strip.

## C — picture first: floating subject card, tool strip with popovers, transport pill
**Fixes:** the picture is the whole window under a 56 px bar (93 %); the only permanent things are the top bar, a 5-icon tool strip and one pill with the transport controls; what you are looking at (Earth, Uranium-238) is a small card that opens when you click the thing, with a summary and "More"; View options open in a popover from the tool strip; in the mockup 18 controls are on screen (with one card and one popover open) instead of today's 139 in the Atom Lab. This is the closest to the AniList target: big visual, space, few sizes, details on click. **Costs:** floating cards and popovers cover part of the picture (the date label and the chart's "protons Z" label had to be avoided by hand; the pill would cover the chart's axis numbers at the bottom, so the chart's version is a small corner pill), and they need dismiss, focus and keyboard rules; everything is behind an icon, so tasks that need many settings at once (setting up an atom) take more clicks and a user must learn the icons (tooltips, a first-run hint); in a small window the cards crowd the picture (`C_*_1000x640`). Most new work: the panels are re-made as cards and popovers (ImGui can do floating windows, but positioning and focus are ours), and the Atom Lab's long lists need a good home (the element picker, an "All settings" sheet).

## Honest comparison and what I would pick
- **AniList test** (big visuals, generous space, few text sizes, details on click): C passes all four; A passes the first three partly (60 % picture, space, 4 sizes) and not the fourth; B passes space and sizes, not the visual.
- **Risk:** A lowest, B medium, C highest. **Atom Lab (many controls):** A copes best, C needs the most design work, B is worst for the chart.
- My suggestion, not a decision: **A as the base, with C's "click the thing → a card opens" for the subject details** (the Earth / nuclide card), because it removes today's worst problems with the least new machinery and can grow towards C later. If the owner wants the AniList feel above all: C. B only if wide pictures were all there was.
- Whatever is picked, DESIGN.md (sizes, buttons, icons, surfaces, long text, themes) is the same for all three.
