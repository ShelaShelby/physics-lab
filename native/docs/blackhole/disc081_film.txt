The Film look's numbers from James et al. 2015 (looks.h): spin 0.6, disc from 9.26 to 18.70 GM/c^2, one temperature 4500 K, no colour or brightness shift,
white balance at 6500 K. The streaks (gas.h StreakField, c = 1.3) and the clump (BL06) of the app, at one moment (T = 0), no light travel time (the app's
picture has it; left out here on every panel alike). No sky.
Exposure and glow, every panel alike: the screen's white is the film disc's own colour (its largest channel, 0.3494 x the Sun's surface; the app's Film rule),
each crossing's light over it and scaled into the screen as the shaders do, then the lab's own glow (looks.h glowReference, strength 0.6).

Read: the hard rims are gone; the lower ring round the shadow shows through the near side (bottom right) and the far side's image through the gaps between
the streaks; the light varies twice as much across the disc (spread of ln Y 1.70 against 0.88): not flat. At the same exposure the new disc is darker
(mean ln Y about 1.1 lower, a third of the light): a crossing covers 1 - e^-1.5 = 0.78 of a pixel where S = 1, and less where the streaks are dark. The
Film exposure is unchanged (its white is the film's colour), so the app's Film picture is darker by the same amount.

Read: at this size the three differ little. 0.06 keeps the disc's edges a little sharper. 0.2 fades 57 % of the disc's width (2 x 0.2 of its 0.70 in ln r)
and dims it further (mean ln Y 0.25 lower than 0.12). None is clearly better, so 0.12, the prototype's choice, is kept.
