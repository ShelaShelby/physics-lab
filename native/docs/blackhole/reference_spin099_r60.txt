Black Hole Lab reference picture round a spinning hole (0.55.2): reference_spin099_r60.png, 1280 x 800
=====================================================================================================
Made by build/blackhole_picture --spin (tests/blackhole_picture.cpp) from the physics module src/blackhole/ (no graphics card); the PNG was then
re-compressed losslessly (same pixels) to stay under 2 MB (1.66 MB).

What it shows: the preset "A feeding black hole (model)" (100 million Suns, the standard thin disc of Page and Thorne 1974 out to 30 GM/c^2, shining at 0.1
of the Eddington limit) with the hole spinning at 0.99 of the largest possible spin, the top of the lab's Spin control; the gas orbits with the spin, in its
plane. No real hole: the spin of Sagittarius A* and M87* is not measured, and this one is a model. In front of OUR sky as seen from Earth.
  Camera: a FIDO (James et al. 2015 (A.3): no angular momentum, carried round by the dragged space once every 6.9e5 GM/c^3, about 10.7 years for this mass)
  at 60 GM/c^2, 10 degrees from the disc's plane, looking straight at the hole.
  The shadow: 8.68 degrees wide, 8.91 high, flattened on the side moving towards the camera (right) and shifted towards the other side.
  The disc: its inner edge at the innermost stable orbit for this spin, 1.4545 GM/c^2 (Bardeen, Press & Teukolsky 1972 (2.21)); hottest ring 169,900 K at
  1.975 GM/c^2; 26.4 % of the infalling mass turned into light. Over the hole the far side bent into view, in front the near side, round the shadow the
  underside and thinner rings. The shift g of each ray from Cunningham 1975 (A9); the approaching side (right) is bluer and much brighter.
  Colour: each ring a black body (sigma T^4 = F, Page and Thorne at this spin), seen at g T, its colour by the CIE 1931 observer; true colour.
  Exposure: the lab's automatic one: the brightest channel of the disc in the picture (1,759 times the Sun's surface luminance) at full brightness, so
  most of the disc looks dim beside its hot inner edge on the approaching side.
  Rays: 2 x 2 per pixel through the fine spin table (192 azimuths x 384 x 2 rays, filled by the ray integrator in double precision, 147,456 rays, 11 s on
  4 cores here), in double; the sky through the table's far directions; stars by inverting the table (747 images drawn, 68 hidden behind the disc).
  Not drawn: the camera's own shift of colour and brightness (a number on the lab's card).

The model's limits: flat, thin, opaque, each face a perfect black body, steady; no turbulence, hot spots, jets or wind; no light from inside the inner
edge; no light of the disc falling back on itself; a disc orbiting against the spin, a tilted or a warped disc are not in the lab. The table's own limits
(REPORT 0.55.2): far directions within 2e-3 rad of the integrator from 1e-3 of the shadow's radius outward; near the disc's plane at this spin the disc's
radius within about 10 % where rays graze the plane.
