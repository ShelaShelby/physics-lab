moving_clump_lightcurve.png (0.56.3 research; PLAN_MOVINGGAS.md)
What it shows: how much of the clump's light reaches the camera, against far-away time, over two orbits: the direct image (orange), the
secondary image under the hole (blue), both (dark), and grey dashed: both if light took no time to travel (every part of the picture at
one moment, shifted by the average travel time so the curves line up).
Same settings as moving_clump_strip.png (spin 0.6, camera 60 GM/c^2 away and 10 degrees above the disc, clump at 6 GM/c^2).
Read: the bright peak is the clump coming towards the camera (Doppler effect). The secondary image peaks a little EARLIER than the direct
one, as Cunningham & Bardeen 1973 describe for an observer away from the disc's plane (p. 254; their hole spins at 1, ours 0.6). With the true travel time the peak is
higher and narrower: different parts of the picture show the gas at different past moments (up to 0.17 of an orbit apart across the
direct image, and the secondary image shows the clump 0.03 to 0.34 of an orbit further in the past). This is why the plan keeps the
exact travel time per pixel.
Real: as in moving_clump_strip.txt. Model: the clump (a choice). Units: 1 = the clump's average light.
