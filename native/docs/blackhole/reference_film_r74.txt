What it shows: the Film look, the choices that O. James, E. von Tunzelmann, P. Franklin and K. S. Thorne, Class. Quantum Grav. 32, 065001 (2015), arXiv:1502.03808
("J15"), state for the black hole and disc of the film Interstellar, drawn on the lab's calculated hole. Every choice and its place in the paper (arXiv v2 pages):
  Spin a/M = 0.6: the film's (J15 section 4.1.1 p. 26, section 1.2 p. 5, App. A.3.1 p. 39); the story needs a/M close to 1, slowed "for visual purposes".
  The disc: thin, opaque, in the plane of the spin, ONE temperature 4500 K all over, a black body (section 4.1.1 p. 26); not feeding. Its edges 9.26 and 18.70 GM/c^2:
    the disc of the paper's Figure 13 (caption p. 24), which the paper also uses for its artists' disc (Figure 14, "the same geometry"; Figure 15a at a/M = 0.6).
  No colour shift and no brightness shift from the gas's speed and gravity (Figure 15a, Figure 16's caption; section 4.2): the disc is one colour everywhere.
  The colour: the paper's white balance, a 6500 K black body with equal red, green and blue (App. A.6 p. 43); the black body by the CIE 1931 observer into sRGB
    (the app's rule for every black body) with one gain per channel, in place of the film stock's colour curves the paper does not give.
  The glow: the app's OWN (blackhole/looks.h): the light above half of the screen's white, in 4 x 4 pixel blocks, blurred by a Gaussian of 1/80 of the picture's
    height and 0.6 of it added back. The film's flare was the measured point-spread function of its IMAX lenses (section 4.3.1), not published: not copied.
  Camera: the paper's view (Figure 13's caption): 74.1 GM/c^2 from the hole, 86.56 degrees from the spin axis (3.44 degrees above the disc), hovering;
    the field of view, 30 degrees across the picture's height, is the lab's choice (the paper gives none).
Not from the paper, the app's own: the sky behind (ours, seen from Earth: the Gaia map and the catalogue stars, looking towards the galactic centre), the exposure
(the disc's brightest channel at full brightness: the screen's white = 0.349 x the Sun's surface luminance), the glow. The mass (100 million Suns, the lab's
feeding preset) does not change the picture; the paper gives no mass for the film's hole.
NOT in the picture: the artists' textures, the real lens flare, the film's colour grading and its "embellishments" (section 4.2 p. 28) are not published. This is a
copy of the paper's stated choices, not of the film's pictures; no picture, frame or texture of the film was used.
Rays: 2 x 2 per pixel through the fine spin table (147,456 rays filled by the ray integrator in double precision), in double; stars by inverting the table
(111 images drawn, 13 hidden behind the disc), drawn over the glow as the app draws them.
