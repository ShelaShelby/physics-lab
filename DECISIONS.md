# Decisions (made by the owner)

- The native app is the main version; Windows only; Orbit Lab and Atom Lab only.
- Versions are labelled alpha until the 1.0 beta, then plain release.
- Real planets start from the JPL Horizons state of 1 January 2026 12:00 TDB (the data, tool and .inc files of 0.10.3 stage 1). (3 Oct 2026)
- The relativity tick box in Real defaults to OFF: measured against JPL (0.10.3 stage 1 report) the Sun-potential GR term moves every planet's position away from JPL (Mercury, worst case 1800-2100: 70,044 km with it, 29,230 km without; it gets the perihelion turn right but the motion along the orbit slightly wrong). The box stays and its note says so. (3 Oct 2026)
- Real planet physics stays an option (default Simple) until the owner has tried it on the PC (the list is in OWNER-TODO.md); the default is decided after that. (3 Oct 2026)
- The Moon in Real mode will come from ELP2000-82B, checked against Horizons (3 Oct 2026).
- Stay on OpenGL 3.3; a newer version only for a speed job later, with a fallback.
- Electron cloud defaults: style Glow, density High.
- "Reset to today" keeps the zoom, the Moons/Dwarfs/Comets/Belts switches and Time speed; it resets the selection and Follow.
- Theme picker is a 3-column grid with the description as a tooltip.
- The Milky Way band is smooth and labelled as simplified.
- Code signing comes later; for now the release text explains the Windows warning.
- No connectors, plugins or extra skills for now (they cost usage).
- Coding sessions run on Sonnet 5.5; the strongest model is used only in a fresh short planning chat (for example the 1.0 design plan).
- More electrons: the first level (shells filling with simplified screening, Slater's rules) needs the owner's decision before it is planned.
- Tests never compare raw bytes of floating-point results recorded on another compiler or platform (cos, sin, exp, log and pow may differ in the last bit between GCC and MSVC). They compare two ways of computing in the same run (exact is right there), or recorded numbers with a stated tolerance, as the stage 2 dot tests do (4 Oct 2026).
- The cloud jump's dots are built over 4 frames and the picture shows a jump only when they are built (the first frames show the cloud as it was, the jump is then under 1 % through); the random numbers the build needs are skipped exactly at the start, so nothing after a jump changes. A speed change must not move any saved number (4 Oct 2026).
- The Atom Lab's speed risks (audit findings 10-12) are first made measurable, not fixed: the benchmark has 11 extra scenes behind a tick box, and the fixes are decided from the owner's numbers (worst frame per scene) from the PC (4 Oct 2026).
- The nucleus's mass in the Atom Lab is Z m_p + N m_n − B/c² with B from the AME2020 binding table; nuclides beyond the table keep A u − Z m_e. The spectrum tests compare with the Rydberg formula by hand and with NIST ASD within (Zα)² (4 Oct 2026).
- The Atom Lab says "non-relativistic" at every Z and warns that its error is large from Z = 27 (where the Dirac 1s energy is about 1 % off), in the readout, the hint and the spectrum note alike. Visible light is 380-780 nm everywhere (a convention the app picked). The sampled average radius is shown only with the full 12,000 dots and is called an estimate "usually within 10 %" (4 Oct 2026).
- A superposition of two overlapping states (the same state twice, a real orientation with an m state of its shell) is renormalised, not refused; its Mix percentages are then amplitudes, not exact shares, and the readout says so. Orthogonal pairs keep exact shares (4 Oct 2026).
- The Atom Lab shows ONE energy per atom: the reduced-mass level −Rydberg(Z, N)·Z²/n², in every view; the Orbit view's own ½v² − Z/r is scaled to it. The Orbit view's leapfrog is split so that no step turns more than 2π/300 rad (the accuracy the default speed has). Scale Fixed is the n = 4 size (4 Oct 2026).
- The Atom Lab uses the CODATA 2022 constants, read from the NIST pages (Bohr radius 5.29177210544e-11 m, Hartree 27.211386245981 eV, atomic unit of time 2.4188843265864e-17 s, and the rest listed in atom_core.h). The web version's rounded 0.529 Å, 27.211 eV and 24.19 as are no longer followed; the saved web results in the tests are rescaled by the constant ratios (4 Oct 2026).
- The lab uses the IAU astronomical unit, 149,597,870,700 m exactly (IAU 2012 Resolution B2; JPL's Astrodynamic Parameters page states this value and names the resolution B1). The web version's 149.6e9 m is no longer followed (3 Oct 2026).
- The Atom Lab's radial graph is P(r) = r²|R_nl(r)|² (area 1, so P(r) dr is the probability between r and r + dr), normalised by the closed form 2n(n+l)!/(n−l−1)!, not by its own quadrature. Its marks are the highest maximum, the average distance a0(3n² − l(l+1))/2Z (one function, `meanRadius`, shared with the readout) and the outline's rmax / Z, which is the outline's farthest reach and in general not the radius holding 90 % of P(r) (the card says so). While a superposition is shown the graph is hidden with an explanation, not drawn, because the mixed state's distance distribution is not one fixed curve for every pair; during a photon jump the card keeps its place and waits (5 Oct 2026).
- The real NIST ASD spectrum is shown as two separate comparisons, never one list: (a) the hydrogen-like ion of the element, which is what the lab models and which validates it (the lab is checked against ASD's components widened by (Zα)², and for heavy ions the gap is the Dirac correction); (b) the neutral atom, which has Z electrons and shows the lab's limit. Data are vacuum wavelengths fetched once (2026-10-04) into data/asd and baked in; a selection only (ion: both levels n ≤ 6; atom: the 30 strongest observed lines with an intensity in 100-2500 nm). ASD's intensities are qualitative and only meaningful within one spectrum, so they are ranked and drawn within one spectrum; a line without one is drawn short and equal (5 Oct 2026).
- The Atom Lab's relativity is an option, off by default (Settings key `dirac`, saved only when on): the exact Dirac (Sommerfeld) energy for a point nucleus, level (n, j), used for the lines and levels of the two spectrum cards ONLY; the pictures, readouts, jump photons and beats stay non-relativistic and the app says so. Reduced mass: the lab's own reduced-mass level plus the relativistic correction of an infinitely heavy nucleus (no source for the full recoil terms was read; the difference to putting the reduced mass into the whole Dirac formula is 9e-9 of the hydrogen Lyman-alpha energy). The Lamb shift, other QED and the nuclear size are not modelled and are shown as the leftover against NIST (4 Oct 2026).
- Test references for the Atom Lab never use `long double` (80 bits on Linux, 64 on MSVC) and never subtract nearly equal numbers: they use double-double arithmetic (error-free transformations and std::fma) pinned by values typed from Python decimal. The lab's Dirac formulas are written without such subtractions (delta = (Z alpha)^2 / ((j + 1/2) + sqrt(...)), E = -m c^2 t / (s (1 + s)), and the correction worked out directly). A change like this is a correction of the arithmetic, not of the physics, and is reported with the old and new numbers (4 Oct 2026).
- Lifetimes and jump rates in the Atom Lab come ONLY from NIST ASD's transition probabilities A with ASD's own accuracy code; the lab adds them (tau = 1/sum A over the allowed jumps to a lower n, share = A/sum A) and never calculates a rate. Where ASD has no A the card says so and shows no number; a state with a partial set gets only an upper limit; a state with no allowed way down (2s) gets no lifetime and a plain note that it still decays by a process ASD does not list. Where ASD's own typing disagrees with the selection rule (Cs LV 1s-2s listed as allowed) the lab follows the rule and says so (4 Oct 2026).
- The Atom Lab draws a nucleus at its MEASURED size, permanently (no setting): the IAEA table of measured rms charge radii (fetched 2026-10-04), drawn as a uniform ball of radius sqrt(5/3) x rms (the rule's ball and a measurement are only comparable in the same quantity: the rule 1.2 A^(1/3) fm is an edge radius, the measurement an rms; a uniform ball has rms sqrt(3/5) x its radius). Where there is no value (and for the 48 isotopes with only a PRELIMINARY value, and for hydrogen-1, which keeps the CODATA 2022 proton radius) the rule stays and the card says so. A charge radius is a size of the protons' charge, not of the matter: halo nuclei and the nucleus's shape wait for data the owner downloads (4 Oct 2026).
- The Atom Lab's nucleus SHAPE comes from a measured ground-state electric quadrupole moment (IAEA recommended values, 2026-10-04) where one can be used: signed, a firm spin of at least 1, and a conversion that needs |beta2| of at most 0.6. Otherwise it keeps the lab's B(E2) value (11 isotopes) or its estimate, and the card and the readout say which of the three the nucleus on screen uses. The table's spectroscopic moment is converted to the intrinsic one with the strong-coupling relation K = I, which the app calls a model assumption; the 0.6 limit is the lab's own (no source) and the owner may change it. A missing moment never means round: spin 0 and 1/2 nuclei have no spectroscopic moment whatever their shape (every even-even nucleus is spin 0). A deformed nucleus keeps its volume and still gives the measured charge radius. Doubly magic nuclei, drawn round by expectation, are now labelled an estimate, not a measurement. Unsigned values, tentative signs and tentative spins are never used for a shape. Magnetic moments are reachable for a later hyperfine stage (4 Oct 2026).
- Atom Lab, Auto photons with no possible jump (0.11 stage 1, chosen by the session, owner may overrule): the lab says why ONCE per reason (the same words as the Emit/Absorb buttons), again only if the state changes or Auto is switched off and on; Auto stays on (it is the user's setting, and it resumes by itself on a real orientation). Whether photon jumps from a complex state or superposition should be allowed is NOT decided: options in native/REPORT.md, waiting for the owner.
- The chart of nuclides (0.11 stage 2, chosen by the session, owner may overrule): the colour is the kind of decay with the largest total odds (a beta-delayed branch counts as its beta); stable means what the lab's table calls stable; where odds are not measured the colour is the kind if all listed modes agree, else grey; nothing is shown as a number where NUBASE has only an extrapolation (#). Spins and the kind of half-life come from NUBASE2020. While the chart is shown the chosen nucleus does not decay on its own and Auto photons wait. Arrow keys move only onto an observed nuclide (so from hydrogen-1 'up' does nothing: helium-2 does not exist). Magic number 126 has a column but no row (the chart ends at 118 protons).
- The chart's landscape (0.11 stage 3, chosen by the session, owner may overrule): height = log10(half-life in seconds) from 10^-23 s to 10^32 s (the table's shortest and longest, rounded out), 12 tiles per scale height (chosen by looking at a software replay: 36 hid the proton-rich side behind the ridge); stable nuclides on one plateau 1.1 above the top (including the 69 NUBASE flags with a mark); no measured half-life, an extrapolation or p-unst = no height (hollow or pale flat); a limit or an approximate value = a pale block at that height (the lab's '~' values too). Isometric view from the neutron-rich, low-Z side (yaw 45 degrees, tipped 54.74 degrees from straight down); hover and click pick the topmost drawn block; the view starts flat (tilt 0) so stage 2's chart is what you see until you tilt it.
- The chart's second height and the island (0.11 stage 4, chosen by the session, owner may overrule): the binding energy per nucleon from AME2020 on a LINEAR scale, bottom 0 (an honest baseline), top 9,000 keV (a round number above the data's highest, which is read from the table at run time), 28 tiles per scale height (chosen by looking); values AME2020 marks as estimates (#, 1,008 nuclides, all of Z 111-118) have no height (flat, pale), as stage 3's extrapolated half-lives. The picture's fit no longer depends on the height mode, so switching does not move it (the half-life landscape is about 8 % smaller than in stage 3 in height-limited windows). The VALLEY is named on the chart (median stable tile) and in the notes box as measured. The ISLAND of stability is stated as a prediction, not seen, disputed, and NOT DRAWN: a source for it could not be reached, and the brief says not to draw or quote from memory. Revisit when the owner sends a source.
- Decay chains on the chart (0.12 stage 1, chosen by the session, owner may overrule): every way a nuclide can decay is drawn (the graph is built once per chosen nuclide); the likeliest way at each nuclide (largest listed share, first listed on a tie) is thick and solid, other ways of at least 0.1 % are thinner, ways below 0.1 % are faint hairlines, and where NUBASE2020 marks the odds as not measured NO way is called likeliest and each is thin and labelled with a ?. A nuclide is timed only when the table has a measured or an approximate half-life (the stage 3 height kinds Measured and Approx); a limit, an extrapolation or no half-life gets NO time, and the chain stops there with the reason (a cross on the chart). The listed odds are added up to 1 (the table lists, for uranium-238, alpha 1 and fission 5.4e-7). Unmeasured odds are listed by the lab's table with equal shares; the walk uses those as a stand-in and marks every such step, instead of stopping (the alternative, stopping at every unmeasured branch, would end most chains early). A split (SF, B-SF, B+SF) or a daughter that is not a nuclide of the table ends a chain. The walk is separate from the chosen nuclide, which stays where the user put it; it shows a step when the user presses the button or, in auto-walk, every 1.6 s of screen time; the drawn lifetimes are never played in real time and each step states how many times faster or slower than real time it was shown. The lab's own decay clock (other views) now uses the same two draws and the same normalised odds, so a rare way listed after a certain one can happen; it still times the extrapolated half-lives the lab's table holds a number for, which is listed as a finding for 0.12 stage 2.
- The accuracy clean-up (0.12 stage 2, chosen by the session, owner may overrule): (1) a nuclide with no half-life number is described by NUBASE2020's own kind (extrapolated, none, only a limit, approximate, p-unst), never as "seen only fleetingly"; (2) the decay clock times only nuclides with a measured or an approximate half-life, the same rule as the chain: the 369 extrapolated and 11 limit-only nuclides keep their place until the user chooses another, and the readout says why (the alternative, timing them from the estimate and saying so, was rejected because an estimate would then drive what the user sees happen); (3) Kr-78 and Xe-124 follow NUBASE2020 and the mendeleev package (stable, observationally), because the two numbers the lab had are in neither source and no source for them could be read; the owner can restore them with a source (OWNER-TODO); (4) the unsourced magic numbers Z = 114 and N = 184 are KEPT and labelled, not removed, because removing them would change the lab's shape estimate for 286 nuclides, which is a change of the model and was outside this stage; (5) the superheavy half-lives take NUBASE2020's values exactly (113 rounded ones and 16 from an older evaluation), so no half-life is called approximate unless NUBASE2020 says so (6 are); (6) the odds of 644 nuclides are said to be incomplete where NUBASE2020 lists a mode with a question mark and no intensity; the lab does not guess the share.
- The 0.13 "GPU update" (owner's decision, 5 Oct 2026): repeated per-item work moves from the CPU to the GPU across the whole app so it runs on old PCs (target: a 2013 Core i7-4770K). Stage 1 (phase colours) stays on OpenGL 3.3 core. From stage 2 the owner plans OpenGL 4.3 with compute shaders; Intel HD 4600 (Haswell, 2013), GeForce 600 and Radeon HD 7000 or newer support it, older PCs will not run the app, and the owner accepts that. The old CPU colouring path is KEPT (Performance card switch "GPU colours", default On, and benchmark scenes 23 and 24) as the reference the new path is tested against and so the owner can compare both on real hardware. (Chosen by the session within that decision: the dot carries its colour INDEX, worked out on the CPU by `phaseBin` as before, not its raw phase, because a 32-bit float phase on the GPU could fall in the neighbouring bin within about 1e-7 of a bin edge, and jumps and superpositions work their indices out on the CPU anyway; the draw order of the old scan is kept with a counting sort because the dots are alpha-blended and the order changes the pixels.)
- 0.13 stage 2, OpenGL 4.3 with a 3.3 fallback (the owner's plan, details chosen by the session, owner may overrule): the app asks for a 4.3 core context first (with and without multisampling), then 3.3 as before; on 3.3, or if the compute shader does not compile or a 4.3 function is missing, there is no compute object and the CPU paths run, with the same picture; the 4.3 functions are loaded separately (`loadGL43`) so a 3.3 PC never needs them. The Performance card and the benchmark report say which way runs. A SECOND switch ("GPU superposition and jumps", `AtomLab::gpuCompute`, default On, not saved) is kept apart from stage 1's "GPU colours", so each stage can be compared alone; benchmark scenes 25 to 28 switch only it off.
- What moved and what did not (0.13 stage 2, chosen after measuring, REPORT.md has the table): the CPU time of a frame in the jump and superposition scenes is mostly the colour group of every dot (thinning, phase, bin), 2 to 5 ms; each dot's amplitudes were already computed once when the dot was made, so that is not repeated per frame. A compute shader now makes the colour groups from the stored amplitudes; the CPU reads back one byte a dot and draws as in stage 1 (projection, depth bands, draw order, upload stay on the CPU). Chosen because it keeps the old draw order (alpha blending is order-dependent), covers every view and style at once, and keeps the picture the same. The cost is a wait for the GPU each frame (an unmeasured assumption: small, the GPU is nearly idle) and the remaining CPU work (about a plain orbital's). The jump's own dot building (7 ms in the frame a jump starts) is NOT moved: only about a third of it is wave-function evaluation (4 evaluations of 0.05 microseconds a dot against 0.6 microseconds in all); the rest is the Metropolis chain of random steps, which is sequential.
- FLOAT PRECISION (0.13 stage 2, the owner's rule, numbers chosen by the session): the GPU works in 32-bit floats. A dot may come out differently from the 64-bit CPU path ONLY if it is within float rounding of a threshold: for a thinning decision |u - w/2| <= 1.5e-6 (w/2) (S/|c| + 1) + 3.1e-8 (u: the dot's fixed random number, 24 bits on the GPU; w: its odds; S/|c|: the cancellation factor |aA| + |bB| over |aA + bBe^(-i theta)|), for a colour bin the phase's distance to a bin edge <= 12 eps S/|c| + 2e-6 rad (eps = 2^-24), for a jump dot's fraction 2e-5. The test counts every differing dot and fails if one is outside its bound or if more than 0.05 % differ. Measured on llvmpipe: none in the benchmark scenes at the five beat times, 3 in 1.9 million dot-frames of a running superposition (worst at 46 % of its bound), many when dots are put on an edge on purpose (all within 19 % of the bound). A real driver may round differently; this was not tested.
- The lab's orbital normalisation (`orbitalNorm`, midpoint rule with 400 points in cos theta) leaves the amplitude too big by 3.1e-6 for 2p and 1.6e-5 for 3d z2 against the exact textbook value 1 (found by the stage 2 physics test). NOT changed in stage 2 ("the physics must not change"); reported. The textbook test states these sizes as its tolerance. Owner to decide whether to fix it (it changes every stored amplitude by up to 1.6e-5).
- 0.13 stage 3, the outline on the GPU (chosen by the session after measuring, owner may overrule): MEASURED (CPU, REPORT.md): first use of an outline is about 30 ms = 13 ms density grid + 17 ms sort to find the 90 % level (+ about 1 ms norm and line building); the Slice frame is 1.86 ms of which 1.69 ms is the 200 x 200 density values. DECIDED: (1) the density grid goes to a compute shader; (2) the 90 % LEVEL is also found ON THE GPU, by bisection over the float bit patterns in a single work group (31 passes over the 262,144 cells, no sort; exact: the level is the value of a cell, the same cell the CPU's sort-and-add stops at, up to the float sum), instead of reading the whole grid back and sorting on the CPU (which would have kept the 17 ms); (3) the grid IS read back, ONCE per orbital (1 MB), because the line building (side view, 15 wireframe planes, mass, reach: about 1 ms) stays on the CPU with exactly the old code, so the lines are the CPU's own code on the GPU's grid; (4) NO per-frame CPU wait (stage 2's wait for the dot groups is not repeated): a job with a fence, polled with a zero timeout each frame; the outline appears a frame or two after it is requested (the benchmark's scene 16 should lose its hitch; during that time no outline is drawn, and the 3.3 PC's CPU path draws it at once, with the old hitch); (5) the Slice's 200 x 200 values are made the same way with ONE job in flight, so while the cut moves the contour is a frame behind the cut (a visible change in behaviour on the GPU path: the contour trails the dots by a frame; the CPU path and the switch Off are unchanged); moving only the values and keeping the marching squares (0.17 ms) on the CPU needs no GPU-side line drawing. The radial graph's 90 % mark is pending the same way (no wait in the panel). A THIRD switch (`AtomLab::gpuOutline`, default On, not saved, Performance card) is kept apart from the other two; scenes 29 and 30 switch only it off. FLOAT RULE as in stage 2: the grid cells agree within 2e-4 relative above 0.1 % of the maximum and 2e-6 of the maximum elsewhere (measured 1.1e-5 and 4.3e-5; near a radial node the polynomial cancels and float error is amplified); the GPU level must enclose 0.9 on its own grid (>= 0.9 - 1e-6) with the next higher cell value enclosing less, and agree with the CPU's to 1e-4 (measured 9e-7).
- EXACT ORBITAL NORMALISATION (0.13 stage 3, the owner's decision, a physics fix on purpose): `orbitalNorm` was a numerical sum (midpoint rule, 400 points in cos theta and 0.01 steps in r) that left the wave functions too big by 3.1e-6 (2p) to 4.4e-5 (4f z3) in amplitude (stage 2 found 3.1e-6 and 1.6e-5; the owner's checker 3.125e-6 and 1.5625e-5 for 2p and 3d z2, which the hand values here confirm). It is now the closed form: radial (n/2)^(2l+3) 2n (n+l)!/(n-l-1)! (generalised Laguerre orthogonality and recurrence) times the sphere integral of each angular polynomial squared (exact rational multiples of pi), see outline.cpp. The formulas are the standard ones written from knowledge; the DLMF and Abramowitz-Stegun chapter references were not opened in this session. Every orbital (all n, l, real and complex, Z = 1, 2, 5) integrates to 1 within 3.1e-13 by an independent quadrature. What it moved: only the recorded jump amplitude sums in test_atom.cpp (4 numbers, each shown to move by the factor the fix predicts), and the outline level by the same 1e-5 order (inside the grid's own resolution).
- 0.13 stage 4, what moved and what did not (chosen by the session after measuring, owner may overrule; REPORT.md has the table): per frame on this machine, Orbit Lab scene 1 spends about 0.7 of 4.1 ms on the rocks (position 0.62, camera 0.10) and scene 6 about 6 of 9.8 ms; the other 3.4 ms is the sky, Milky Way and trails (about 86,000 vertices pushed every frame), NOT rocks, and is left (ROADMAP, Later). DECIDED: rocks and the chart's static geometry go to the GPU; the Cloud 2D buffer is fixed on the CPU (kept vector, no copy into the renderer); trails, walker, hover, legend stay as they are.
- ROCK PRECISION RULE and the Kepler solver (0.13 stage 4, the owner's brief, numbers chosen by the session): a rock is a Kepler ellipse (no planet pulls, the Oort cloud does not move); the physics is the same on the CPU and the GPU. Anything that grows without limit stays in DOUBLE: the simulated time t is sent as two floats (hi, lo: exact), the mean anomaly M = M0 + n t is formed with the mean motion n as a double (made on the CPU, one function for both paths) and brought into -pi..pi IN DOUBLE (GLSL 4.00 double precision, which every OpenGL 4.3 context has; a shader that does not compile leaves the CPU path); the Trojans' sway phase is reduced in double once per frame; only the small angle goes into float. Kepler's equation: CPU (orbit::solveKepler) Newton from M + e sin M (from +-pi for e > 0.8), at least 3 steps, stop when a step moves E by less than 1e-9, at most 60, bisection safety net; GPU: the same in float, at least 3 steps, stop below 5e-7, at most 20, no net (from +-pi Newton does not run away). FINDING: up to stage 3 the belts used exactly 3 steps: exact for the app's rocks (largest e 0.498) but 5.8e-3 rad off at e = 0.9, 0.45 rad at 0.99, runaway at 0.999; the app makes no such rock, the fix changes the app's rocks by under 1e-9 of a. Bound of the GPU against the CPU: 0.01 point on the picture for every rock within 3,000 points (measured 0.0025), and 3e-4 of the coordinate farther out (float cancellation in D - depth near the perspective limit); against the textbook (bisection): 2e-7 + 4e-7/(1 - e) of a (measured 5e-7 at worst, e = 0.99). A rock far off the picture is not culled on the GPU (the rasteriser clips it).

- FLOATING WINDOWS ARE SOLID, NOT CLEAR (0.14 stage 1.1, found by the owner on the real app): in Frutiger Aero the Learn cards and the Orbit Lab picture showed through the guide and changelog windows. Cause: stage 1 made every window background see-through and only some windows were then painted; the stage 1 contrast rule only looked at glass over the page gradient, never over real content. DECISION: every window and pop-up that holds text hides what is behind it. A floating window paints the page gradient (opaque) and then the glass (alpha 0.94) over it, so the glass look comes from the gradient, the shine, the rim and the shadow, not from transparency; a pop-up list (combo, tooltip) is one solid colour; only the docked panels (header, side panels, tab page) are see-through, because behind them there is only the page. If the owner wants a blur later it is its own task. - AERO'S REAL LOOK AND Y2K, THE OWNER'S REFERENCES (0.14 stage 2, the owner's brief, numbers chosen by the session): Aero is saturated (page: sky blue, aqua #19b5d6, leaf green #8fd63f; buttons aqua jelly, chosen buttons lime jelly #6cc419; Vista-style deep blue title bars with a shine band; a bright rim and a soft aqua glow so windows read over the dark picture; clouds and glass bubbles on the page). Y2K = the cyber / techno PRINT look, not rainbow bubbles: Y2K Ice (light) and Y2K Night (dark), nearly monochrome icy blue, cold white, black and brushed silver, violet only as a small light streak (Night); fine grain, halftone dots and grid lines on the page, thin HUD rules, corner brackets and a tiny "SYS 0.14" label as decoration; chrome buttons with a cold highlight line; silver or dark title bars. Style only: no logos, characters, photos or artwork of any company. Not secret themes.
- TEXTURES ARE UNDER THE GLASS AND BOUNDED (0.14 stage 2): every page texture (clouds, bubbles, grain, halftone, grid, streak) is drawn on the page layer, under the glass of docked panels, never over text, and each has a number in `PageFx` = the most it can move a pixel of the page; the contrast tests use the page moved by the sum of them (capped at 1) towards the texture colour, and by the streak towards violet. A picture test measures the real strongest change against that bound (Ice 32 of a bound 79, Night 75 of 156, out of 255). Limits chosen: each texture at most 12 %, all together at most 32 %.
- CHROME BUTTONS ARE DARK IN NIGHT (0.14 stage 2): text on a button face must pass 4.5:1 at the brightest point of the gloss (the top highlight, the glint, the brush lines), so Night's chrome is dark gunmetal with a cold highlight line and an icy rim, not bright silver; Ice has the bright silver. If the owner wants bright silver buttons in Night, button text would have to become dark there (a per-theme button text colour: not built).

- THEME EFFECTS SWITCH (0.14 stage 3, the owner's brief, details chosen by the session): Settings → Theme effects On / Off, saved as `effects=on|off`, default On. Off removes only what is pure decoration: page textures (clouds, bubbles, grain, halftone, grid, streak), glints and brush lines on buttons, the reflection under windows and cards, corner brackets, HUD rules and the spec label. Colours, the gloss gradients, rim, outer glow, shine, shadow, title bars and fonts stay. The plain themes have none of it, so nothing changes for them. The benchmark report prints the setting. Done by one function on the style data (`withoutEffects`), so no drawing code has its own check.
