# Decisions (made by the owner)

- The native app is the main version; Windows only; Orbit Lab and Atom Lab only.
- Versions are labelled alpha until the 1.0 beta, then plain release.
- Real planets start from the JPL Horizons state of 1 January 2026 12:00 TDB (the data, tool and .inc files of 0.10.3 stage 1). (3 Oct 2026)
- The relativity tick box in Real defaults to OFF: measured against JPL (0.10.3 stage 1 report) the Sun-potential GR term moves every planet's position away from JPL (Mercury, worst case 1800-2100: 70,044 km with it, 29,230 km without; it gets the perihelion turn right but the motion along the orbit slightly wrong). The box stays and its note says so. (3 Oct 2026)
- Real planet physics stays an option (default Simple) until the owner has tried it on the PC (the list is in OWNER-TODO.md); the default is decided after that. (3 Oct 2026)
- The Moon in Real mode will come from ELP2000-82B, checked against Horizons (3 Oct 2026).
- Stay on OpenGL 3.3; a newer version only for a speed job later, with a fallback.
- Electron cloud defaults: style Glow, density High.
- "Reset to today" keeps the zoom, the Moons/Dwarfs/Comets/Belts switches and Time speed; it resets the selection and Follow.
- Theme picker is a 3-column grid with the description as a tooltip.
- The Milky Way band is smooth and labelled as simplified.
- Code signing comes later; for now the release text explains the Windows warning.
- No connectors, plugins or extra skills for now (they cost usage).
- Coding sessions run on Sonnet 5.5; the strongest model is used only in a fresh short planning chat (for example the 1.0 design plan).
- More electrons: the first level (shells filling with simplified screening, Slater's rules) needs the owner's decision before it is planned.
- Tests never compare raw bytes of floating-point results recorded on another compiler or platform (cos, sin, exp, log and pow may differ in the last bit between GCC and MSVC). They compare two ways of computing in the same run (exact is right there), or recorded numbers with a stated tolerance, as the stage 2 dot tests do (4 Oct 2026).
- The cloud jump's dots are built over 4 frames and the picture shows a jump only when they are built (the first frames show the cloud as it was, the jump is then under 1 % through); the random numbers the build needs are skipped exactly at the start, so nothing after a jump changes. A speed change must not move any saved number (4 Oct 2026).
- The Atom Lab's speed risks (audit findings 10-12) are first made measurable, not fixed: the benchmark has 11 extra scenes behind a tick box, and the fixes are decided from the owner's numbers (worst frame per scene) from the PC (4 Oct 2026).
- The nucleus's mass in the Atom Lab is Z m_p + N m_n − B/c² with B from the AME2020 binding table; nuclides beyond the table keep A u − Z m_e. The spectrum tests compare with the Rydberg formula by hand and with NIST ASD within (Zα)² (4 Oct 2026).
- The Atom Lab says "non-relativistic" at every Z and warns that its error is large from Z = 27 (where the Dirac 1s energy is about 1 % off), in the readout, the hint and the spectrum note alike. Visible light is 380-780 nm everywhere (a convention the app picked). The sampled average radius is shown only with the full 12,000 dots and is called an estimate "usually within 10 %" (4 Oct 2026).
- A superposition of two overlapping states (the same state twice, a real orientation with an m state of its shell) is renormalised, not refused; its Mix percentages are then amplitudes, not exact shares, and the readout says so. Orthogonal pairs keep exact shares (4 Oct 2026).
- The Atom Lab shows ONE energy per atom: the reduced-mass level −Rydberg(Z, N)·Z²/n², in every view; the Orbit view's own ½v² − Z/r is scaled to it. The Orbit view's leapfrog is split so that no step turns more than 2π/300 rad (the accuracy the default speed has). Scale Fixed is the n = 4 size (4 Oct 2026).
- The Atom Lab uses the CODATA 2022 constants, read from the NIST pages (Bohr radius 5.29177210544e-11 m, Hartree 27.211386245981 eV, atomic unit of time 2.4188843265864e-17 s, and the rest listed in atom_core.h). The web version's rounded 0.529 Å, 27.211 eV and 24.19 as are no longer followed; the saved web results in the tests are rescaled by the constant ratios (4 Oct 2026).
- The lab uses the IAU astronomical unit, 149,597,870,700 m exactly (IAU 2012 Resolution B2; JPL's Astrodynamic Parameters page states this value and names the resolution B1). The web version's 149.6e9 m is no longer followed (3 Oct 2026).
- The Atom Lab's radial graph is P(r) = r²|R_nl(r)|² (area 1, so P(r) dr is the probability between r and r + dr), normalised by the closed form 2n(n+l)!/(n−l−1)!, not by its own quadrature. Its marks are the highest maximum, the average distance a0(3n² − l(l+1))/2Z (one function, `meanRadius`, shared with the readout) and the outline's rmax / Z, which is the outline's farthest reach and in general not the radius holding 90 % of P(r) (the card says so). While a superposition is shown the graph is hidden with an explanation, not drawn, because the mixed state's distance distribution is not one fixed curve for every pair; during a photon jump the card keeps its place and waits (5 Oct 2026).
- The real NIST ASD spectrum is shown as two separate comparisons, never one list: (a) the hydrogen-like ion of the element, which is what the lab models and which validates it (the lab is checked against ASD's components widened by (Zα)², and for heavy ions the gap is the Dirac correction); (b) the neutral atom, which has Z electrons and shows the lab's limit. Data are vacuum wavelengths fetched once (2026-10-04) into data/asd and baked in; a selection only (ion: both levels n ≤ 6; atom: the 30 strongest observed lines with an intensity in 100-2500 nm). ASD's intensities are qualitative and only meaningful within one spectrum, so they are ranked and drawn within one spectrum; a line without one is drawn short and equal (5 Oct 2026).
- The Atom Lab's relativity is an option, off by default (Settings key `dirac`, saved only when on): the exact Dirac (Sommerfeld) energy for a point nucleus, level (n, j), used for the lines and levels of the two spectrum cards ONLY; the pictures, readouts, jump photons and beats stay non-relativistic and the app says so. Reduced mass: the lab's own reduced-mass level plus the relativistic correction of an infinitely heavy nucleus (no source for the full recoil terms was read; the difference to putting the reduced mass into the whole Dirac formula is 9e-9 of the hydrogen Lyman-alpha energy). The Lamb shift, other QED and the nuclear size are not modelled and are shown as the leftover against NIST (4 Oct 2026).
- Test references for the Atom Lab never use `long double` (80 bits on Linux, 64 on MSVC) and never subtract nearly equal numbers: they use double-double arithmetic (error-free transformations and std::fma) pinned by values typed from Python decimal. The lab's Dirac formulas are written without such subtractions (delta = (Z alpha)^2 / ((j + 1/2) + sqrt(...)), E = -m c^2 t / (s (1 + s)), and the correction worked out directly). A change like this is a correction of the arithmetic, not of the physics, and is reported with the old and new numbers (4 Oct 2026).
- Lifetimes and jump rates in the Atom Lab come ONLY from NIST ASD's transition probabilities A with ASD's own accuracy code; the lab adds them (tau = 1/sum A over the allowed jumps to a lower n, share = A/sum A) and never calculates a rate. Where ASD has no A the card says so and shows no number; a state with a partial set gets only an upper limit; a state with no allowed way down (2s) gets no lifetime and a plain note that it still decays by a process ASD does not list. Where ASD's own typing disagrees with the selection rule (Cs LV 1s-2s listed as allowed) the lab follows the rule and says so (4 Oct 2026).
- The Atom Lab draws a nucleus at its MEASURED size, permanently (no setting): the IAEA table of measured rms charge radii (fetched 2026-10-04), drawn as a uniform ball of radius sqrt(5/3) x rms (the rule's ball and a measurement are only comparable in the same quantity: the rule 1.2 A^(1/3) fm is an edge radius, the measurement an rms; a uniform ball has rms sqrt(3/5) x its radius). Where there is no value (and for the 48 isotopes with only a PRELIMINARY value, and for hydrogen-1, which keeps the CODATA 2022 proton radius) the rule stays and the card says so. A charge radius is a size of the protons' charge, not of the matter: halo nuclei and the nucleus's shape wait for data the owner downloads (4 Oct 2026).
- The Atom Lab's nucleus SHAPE comes from a measured ground-state electric quadrupole moment (IAEA recommended values, 2026-10-04) where one can be used: signed, a firm spin of at least 1, and a conversion that needs |beta2| of at most 0.6. Otherwise it keeps the lab's B(E2) value (11 isotopes) or its estimate, and the card and the readout say which of the three the nucleus on screen uses. The table's spectroscopic moment is converted to the intrinsic one with the strong-coupling relation K = I, which the app calls a model assumption; the 0.6 limit is the lab's own (no source) and the owner may change it. A missing moment never means round: spin 0 and 1/2 nuclei have no spectroscopic moment whatever their shape (every even-even nucleus is spin 0). A deformed nucleus keeps its volume and still gives the measured charge radius. Doubly magic nuclei, drawn round by expectation, are now labelled an estimate, not a measurement. Unsigned values, tentative signs and tentative spins are never used for a shape. Magnetic moments are reachable for a later hyperfine stage (4 Oct 2026).
